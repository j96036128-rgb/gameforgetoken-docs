# GameForge Review Guidelines

This document defines how review work is conducted, evaluated, and attributed within the GameForge system.

---

## 1. What Counts as Review Work

Review is first-class contribution. A completed review that meets the standards below is logged in the participation ledger with the same standing as build work.

### Acceptable Review Outcomes

Every review must conclude with one of three outcomes:

- **Accepted:** The submission meets all acceptance criteria specified in the Build Request.
- **Revision Requested:** The submission does not yet meet criteria. The reviewer documents specific, actionable changes required.
- **Rejected:** The submission does not meet criteria and revision is not appropriate. The reviewer documents the reasoning.

### Requirements for a Valid Review

A review is valid when it includes:

1. **Explicit outcome:** One of the three outcomes above.
2. **Written reasoning:** A clear explanation of why the submission does or does not meet acceptance criteria.
3. **Reference to criteria:** Direct reference to specific acceptance criteria from the Build Request.
4. **Actionable feedback:** For revision requests, specific guidance on what must change.

### Thoughtful Rejection as Contribution

A well-reasoned rejection is a contribution. Protecting the ledger from substandard work has value. Rejections that document reasoning clearly and reference acceptance criteria count as review contributions.

---

## 2. How Reviews Are Evaluated

Reviews are evaluated on three dimensions:

### Clarity of Reasoning

- Is the rationale understandable to someone unfamiliar with the submission?
- Does the review avoid ambiguity?
- Is the language precise?

### Alignment with Acceptance Criteria

- Does the review directly reference the Build Request's acceptance criteria?
- Is the outcome consistent with what the criteria specify?
- Does the review avoid introducing criteria not present in the Build Request?

### Constructiveness

- For revision requests: Are the required changes specific and actionable?
- For rejections: Is the reasoning sufficient for the contributor to understand why?
- Does the review avoid unnecessary criticism unrelated to the acceptance criteria?

---

## 3. Attribution

### Ledger Entries

Accepted reviews are logged in the participation ledger with:

- Reviewer identifier
- Build Request ID
- Submission reviewed
- Review outcome
- Date

### Standing

Review work counts equally toward contributor standing:

- Review contributions count toward the 3-contribution threshold for Trusted Contributor status
- Reviewers build standing through review work alone, without shipping build contributions

### Visibility

All reviews and their reasoning are public. Attribution is permanent and immutable once logged.

---

## 4. Conflict of Interest

### Mandatory Recusal

Reviewers must recuse themselves from reviewing:

- Work they contributed to
- Work submitted by someone with whom they have a direct personal or financial relationship
- Any submission where they have prior involvement that could affect impartiality

### Disclosure Requirement

Before beginning a review, the reviewer must disclose any potential conflict of interest. When in doubt, disclose.

### Handling Recusals

When a reviewer recuses themselves:

- Another eligible reviewer is assigned
- If no other reviewer is available, the review is deferred

---

## 5. CRG Role in Reviews

### Validation, Not Content

The Core Review Group (CRG) validates that reviews meet the standards in this document. The CRG does not override review judgments unless:

- The review lacks required elements (outcome, reasoning, criteria reference)
- A dispute is raised through the challenge process defined in GOVERNANCE.md
- A conflict of interest was not disclosed

### Intervention

The CRG intervenes only when:

- A review is incomplete or invalid
- A challenge has been raised
- A conflict of interest is discovered after the fact

The CRG does not substitute its judgment for that of a valid reviewer absent a formal challenge.
